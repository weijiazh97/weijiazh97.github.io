About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Biomedical Engineering at Columbia University, working at the intersection of artificial intelligence, reinforcement learning, and human neuroscience.

My research investigates how humans and artificial agents learn, reason, and adapt in changing environments. I combine human neural recordings, computational modeling, and large language model evaluation to understand the mechanisms underlying flexible intelligence.

I am particularly interested in continual learning, reasoning, and developing AI systems that can adapt to new information without losing previously acquired knowledge.

Research

My research spans human neuroscience, reinforcement learning, and artificial intelligence, with a common focus on learning and adaptation.

LLM Evaluation · Reasoning · Continual Learning

Revaluation in Large Language Models

How do language models adapt their reasoning when previously learned relationships become outdated?

This project investigates reward and transition revaluation in language models, examining how different reasoning strategies and model architectures support flexible adaptation.

Human Neuroscience · Reinforcement Learning

Neural Mechanisms of Flexible Adaptation

How do humans update internal representations when rewards or environmental structure change?

Using human single-neuron recordings, virtual reality, and computational models, this project investigates the neural and computational mechanisms supporting flexible learning and non-local inference.
