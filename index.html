01 / ABOUT

Research Summary.

I am a Ph.D. student in Biomedical Engineering at Columbia University, affiliated with the Center for Theoretical Neuroscience (CTN). I am advised by Joshua Jacobs (University of Chicago) and Kimberly L. Stachenfeld (Columbia University / Google DeepMind).

I study how intelligent systems learn, reason, and adapt as their environments evolve. My research investigates the mechanisms of representation updating and continual learning across biological and artificial systems, and why these mechanisms enable flexible inference and generalization in some settings but produce systematic failures in others.

02 / RESEARCH

Selected research.

My research spans human neuroscience, reinforcement learning, and artificial intelligence, with a common focus on learning and adaptation.

01

LLM EVALUATION / REASONING

Revaluation in Large Language Models

What reasoning strategies do language models use to adapt as information changes across multiple turns?

I develop controlled multi-turn benchmarks to investigate how large language models revise learned associations and update downstream inferences. Drawing on theories of human learning and decision-making, I characterize reasoning strategies across open-weight and closed-source models, examining when they support flexible adaptation or lead to systematic failures.

LLM Evaluation Reasoning Continual Learning Reinforcement Learning
02

HUMAN NEUROSCIENCE / REINFORCEMENT LEARNING

Neural Mechanisms of Flexible Adaptation

How do humans update internal representations when rewards or environmental structure change?

Using human single-neuron recordings, virtual reality, and computational models, this project investigates the neural and computational mechanisms supporting flexible learning and non-local inference.
